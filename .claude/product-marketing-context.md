# Product Marketing Context

*Last updated: 2026-07-11. Drafted for the V2.0 repositioning from music-contractor/string-quartet to broad performing-arts platform. Sources: product code audit, market research (League of American Orchestras, OPERA America, Dance/USA, AFM CBAs), competitive analysis. Verify claims marked (?) before publishing.*

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** The personnel platform for performing-arts organizations — staff every rehearsal and performance without the phone tree.
**What it does:** Podium manages the whole staffing loop for organizations that hire performers per-event: build a roster, create an event with its rehearsals and performances, send offers that performers accept or decline from their phone, cascade to the next person when someone passes, handle substitutions, and track pay (rates, W-9s, 1099s). Performers get a self-service portal for offers, schedules, and files.
**Product category:** Performing-arts personnel / orchestra & ensemble management software (the "who's playing which service" system of record). Adjacent shelves customers search: "orchestra personnel software," "substitute musician management," "choir scheduling," "theatre company management," "gig offer app."
**Product type:** Multi-tenant B2B SaaS.
**Business model:** Per-organization subscription, tiered by roster size / features. Current live model: single $29/mo Pro (billing dormant; founding orgs comped). Planned tiers: Ensemble $29 / Orchestra $79 / Symphony (Institution) $199 — mo, budget-scaled norm for the vertical.

## Target Audience
**Target organizations:** Community & regional orchestras, chamber ensembles, choirs & choral societies, opera companies, ballet/dance companies (pit + casting), theatre companies & school programs, churches with paid musicians, and entertainment/event agencies. Sweet spot: organizations that hire freelancers per-service and are below the OPAS/ArtsVision enterprise price floor (i.e., almost everyone who isn't a major symphony).
**Decision-makers / buyers:** Personnel managers, orchestra/operations managers, contractors ("fixers"), music directors, choir directors, company managers, worship/music pastors, agency owners.
**Primary use case:** Fill dated positions with the right people, fast, and prove who was called in what order — replacing spreadsheets + group texts + phone trees.
**Jobs to be done:**
- When a player drops or a seat opens, help me call subs in the right order and fill it in minutes, not days.
- Let performers accept/decline and request subs themselves so my inbox isn't the bottleneck.
- Keep pay, W-9s, and 1099s straight without a second system.
**Use cases:** season/production staffing; last-minute sub calls; per-service pay runs; W-9 collection; sending gig details & sheet music with confirmations; availability collection before offers.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Staffing performers per-service is still run on spreadsheets, group texts, and phone trees. When someone drops, filling the seat is a manual scramble, and there's no record of who was offered what, when, or in what order.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Enterprise suites (OPAS, ArtsVision, DIESE) are databases of record priced for $7M+ budgets and don't automate offer cascades.
- Volunteer schedulers (Planning Center, Muzodo, Chorus Connection) poll internal members — no concept of paid freelancers, rates, W-9/1099, or sub cascades.
- The young cascade tools (Rhapsody, Ensemble Manager, StageSub) are single-segment and small; none spans the whole performing-arts market affordably.
**What it costs them:** Hours per week of manual calling; last-minute holes; strained relationships when the call list feels arbitrary; pay/tax errors.
**Emotional tension:** The dread of an unfilled seat at the downbeat, and the low-grade guilt/politics of "who gets called first."

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Rhapsody, Ensemble Manager, StageSub — young, single-segment, mostly orchestra-only; some EU-only. Fall short: narrow, not multi-vertical, thin on the affordable-mid-market.
**Secondary (enterprise):** OPAS, ArtsVision, DIESE — full ops suites for major institutions. Fall short: expensive, complex, sales-gated, no automated cascade.
**Secondary (volunteer scheduling):** Planning Center Services (dominant in churches), Chorus Connection, Muzodo. Fall short: volunteers only — no paid-freelancer hiring, offers with rates, or 1099.
**Indirect:** Spreadsheets + group texts + a paper sub list; a personnel manager's phone. Falls short: no cascade, no audit trail, no self-service, error-prone.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Contractor-first: offers with accept/decline, automatic cascade to the next candidate, substitution/backfill, and 1099/W-9/pay tracking — built in, not bolted on.
- One platform, many art forms: configurable per organization type (orchestra, choir, theatre, dance, church, agency) so the words and workflow fit each.
- Affordable and modern: enterprise-grade workflow well below the OPAS price floor.
- Auditable, fair call order: a defensible record of who was offered what, when — something freelancers and unions actually want.
- Performer self-service portal: offers, schedule, availability, files, W-9 — off the manager's inbox.
**Why customers choose us:** It's the only affordable, modern tool that actually *hires* freelancers across performing-arts disciplines — not just a roster, not just volunteer scheduling, not a $10k enterprise suite.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "We already use spreadsheets/texts." | Those don't cascade offers, don't keep an audit trail, and put you in the reply loop for every seat. Podium fills seats while you sleep. |
| "Is it built for *our* kind of group?" | Yes — Podium adapts its terminology and workflow to orchestras, choirs, theatre, dance, church, and agencies. |
| "We can't afford enterprise software." | Podium is a fraction of OPAS/ArtsVision — priced for community and regional budgets, not $7M symphonies. |
| "Switching mid-season is risky." | Import your roster in minutes; start with one production; performers need no training to accept an offer. |
| "Union rules are complicated." (up-market) | Configurable call order (rank/rotation/seniority) with an audit trail; per-service pay modeling on the roadmap. |
**Anti-persona:** Solo touring pop/rock acts and DJ booking (served by Gigwell/Back On Stage); major symphonies fully committed to OPAS; all-volunteer groups needing only availability polling (Muzodo/Planning Center).

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** A blown last-minute sub call; a spreadsheet that broke; a manager quitting and taking the "system" (their head) with them.
**Pull:** Offers that cascade automatically; performers self-serving; one place for pay/W-9; looks professional to the board and the players.
**Habit:** "The phone tree works well enough"; the personnel manager's personal relationships and memory.
**Anxiety:** Migration effort; will performers actually use it; is it really built for our art form.
